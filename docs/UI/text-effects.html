Text Effects

Feature guide · UI / text styling

Any text in the UI can carry a shadow, a glow and an outline. You declare them in CSS, on the rule that styles the text, like any other property. They decorate the glyphs only: text size, wrapping, selection and the caret do not change, and neither does the element's layout box. Labels, buttons, fields and every control that draws text through the shared text path get them, including localized text and fallback fonts.

Declare an effect

Put the colors in tokens on the root element of your layout and read them with var(), so one palette change restyles every effect. Tokens inherit, so every text inside that element reads them. One selector per rule.

.hud {
    --hud_text_shadow: #000000b3;
    --hud_text_glow: #e05a0099;
    --hud_text_outline: #172418;
}

.hud-title {
    color: #eee1b1;
    text-shadow: 0px 2px 1px var(--hud_text_shadow);
    text-outline: 1px var(--hud_text_outline);
}

.hud-selected {
    color: #ffd263;
    text-glow: 6px var(--hud_text_glow);
}

Then give the text its class in the layout:

<UIElement class="hud">
    <Label class="hud-title" text="Adventure"/>
    <Label class="hud-selected" text="Start"/>
</UIElement>

Do not put the tokens on :root

:root matches only an element without a parent. A UI document mounted in a scene or in the editor has a parent, so its :root rules never apply, and the effects draw nothing. Declare the tokens on a class of the layout's own root element.

Everything is CSS

An effect is a style property. It inherits, it responds to :hover and the other pseudo-classes, it transitions, and a hot-reloaded stylesheet changes it at once. There is no C++ call to set one.

Properties

ShorthandValueLonghands

text-glowradius color, or none text-glow-radius, text-glow-color
text-outlinewidth color, or none text-outline-width, text-outline-color
  • Lengths are logical pixels (px or unitless) and scale with the UI scale. Shadow offsets may be negative; blur, radius and width may not.
  • Colors are named, hexadecimal (#rgb, #rrggbb, #rrggbbaa), rgb(), rgba(), hsl() or a var() that resolves to one. A transparent color turns its effect off.
  • Every longhand inherits and accepts initial, inherit and unset. none clears a shorthand.
  • A malformed value is ignored as a whole, and the earlier declaration stays. The log warns once per malformed declaration and names the accepted form. A shadow without a color is one of these: write the color.

What each effect draws

The three effects are drawn under the text's fill, from one measurement: the distance from each pixel to the glyph's outline.

Outline

Covers every pixel within width of the glyph, with the same one-pixel soft edge the glyph has. It grows the glyph outward; the fill is unchanged on top of it.

Glow

Full strength at the glyph's edge (or the outline's edge), fading as exp(-2 (d / radius)²) with the distance d past it, the same falloff as the glow property on boxes. It fades out at twice the radius.

Shadow, sharp

With no blur, the glyph's own shape moved by the offset.

Shadow, blurred

The blur is the CSS blur radius: the shadow's edge is a Gaussian of standard deviation blur / 2, as for box-shadow. It reaches 1.5 × blur past the offset shape.

The shadow and the glow are cast by the outlined glyph: with an outline, they start at the outline's edge. Paint order, back to front: shadow, glow, outline, fill. A line draws the effects of all of its glyphs first and its fills after, so no effect covers a letter of its own line, and every shadow and glow of the line paints under every outline of it. Within the shadow-and-glow layer the effects of neighboring glyphs stack in reading order, so a glyph's shadow can sit over the previous glyph's glow. A semi-transparent text color shows the effects through the glyph. For opacity below 1, see Limits.

Clipping

While its text fits, an element's own overflow: hidden does not cut its text's effects. The clip is the padding box grown by the effect's reach on every side; the text itself stays inside the padding box because it fits (an ellipsized run ends inside the box), so a glowing label in a fixed-height list or tree row keeps its whole glow, ellipsized or not. Glyph ink that overhangs the text's advance, such as an italic's tail, can show past the padding box by up to the reach. The reach is the outline width plus twice the radius for a glow; the larger offset plus the outline width, 1.5 × the blur and 1 pixel of antialiasing for a shadow; and the width plus 1 pixel for an outline. The clips of the element's ancestors still apply: a parent with overflow: hidden cuts the effect at its own edge. A scroll view's viewport is such an ancestor: give the scrolled content padding of at least the reach, or the first and last rows' effects are cut at its edge.

  • Text that the box cuts (it overflows without an ellipsis, or wraps taller than the box) keeps the padding box, and its effects are cut with it.
  • An element whose overflow clip also clips child elements keeps the padding box for its own text's effects.

Limits

  • One shadow per element. Shadow lists and inset are not supported.
  • Color glyphs (emoji) take the shadow only, sharp, from their alpha. They have no outline to measure a glow or an outline from.
  • Outline width, glow radius and shadow blur clamp to 64 device pixels, and each shadow offset to 256 device pixels either way, so an effect reaches at most 417 device pixels past its glyph.
  • Effects are drawn line by line. A line's glow, outline or upward shadow paints over the line above where it reaches that far: a glow reaches twice its radius. Keep that reach within the gap between lines, or raise the line-height.
  • While an element's opacity is below 1, a neighboring glyph's glow shows faintly through the letters: at most opacity × (1 − opacity) of the glow's strength, a quarter at 50%. A glyph's own effects never show through its letters, and at opacity 1 no effect shows through any letter. It shows on dimmed or disabled text styled with opacity and while a glowing label fades in or out. Where that matters, drop the glow in the dimmed rule (text-glow: none). In the same way, below opacity 1 a glyph's glow shows through its own outline at up to opacity × (1 − opacity) of its strength. Issue #2940 tracks drawing the text and its effects as one layer.
  • Effect lengths are absolute pixels (em is not accepted), so they do not grow with the font size: a 1px outline is a hairline at 64px. Choose them for the size the text is shown at, and check the palette over every background the text sits on: a glow that reads over the sky can disappear over bright ground, where a dark shadow and outline still carry the letters.
  • A glyph with an outline or a glow measures its distance to the outline over the curves within reach, so a wider outline or glow costs more per pixel and widens the area each glyph draws. Each glyph is drawn once per layer the text uses: once for plain text, twice with a shadow or a glow only, twice with an outline only, and three times with an outline and a shadow or glow. Text without effects draws exactly as before.

Custom text controls

A control that draws its own text gets the effects of its style when it emits through MakeGlyphRunTarget and EmitGlyphRun: the target carries the element's effects, scaled to device pixels, and EmitGlyphRun draws them as one effect primitive per glyph before the run's fills.
