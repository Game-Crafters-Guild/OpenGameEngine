World Mark-ups

Feature guide · editor / working with an agent

A mark-up is a note on a place in the world: a box or a sphere with a title, a status, tags, a description and a thread of comments. You and an AI agent connected to the editor use mark-ups to talk about the scene by pointing at it. You mark "Village center", the agent reads it and does the work; the agent proposes "Lake" and "Forest" as shapes, you drag them where they belong and set them to Requested.

Mark-ups are editor-only. They are saved in the scene file, so a scene diff shows the conversation, and a game export removes them: the exported scene has no mark-up, no title and no note.

What a mark-up carries

PartWhat it is
TitleThe entity's name.
ShapeA box or a sphere (a place), or a region (an area). The entity's transform places and sizes a box or a sphere: a box's scale is its full size, a sphere's scale is its diameter. Move and scale it with the transform tool like any entity. Regions are described below.
StatusOne status from the project's status group. The defaults: Proposed (the agent drew it and asks), Requested (do the work), InProgress (shown as "In progress"), Complete, Revision (finished work asked for again) and Problem. A project can rename and recolor them.
TagsAny number of free tags from the project's tag vocabulary.
Description and threadText of any length, and comments, each with its author and time. A status change is recorded in the thread, and so is an edit of the mark-up itself (moved, resized, rotated, renamed, a new shape, color or description); edits one author makes within two minutes of each other share one line, such as "You moved and resized it". In the comment field Enter adds the comment and Shift+Enter starts a new line; links in a comment open in the browser, and an entity the agent names shows as a chip: hovering it highlights the entity in the Scene View, a click selects it, and the frame glyph at its end frames it.
Author and updatesWho created it, who changed it last (you or the agent) and when. Your edits count as yours. What the agent does through the editor's debug port counts as the agent's, including an undo it asks for; the exception is input the agent simulates (send_key, click_element, perform_drop), which the editor handles as yours.

Regions

A region is an area of ground: a forest, a district, a river with its banks. Its outline is a closed spline of 3 to 256 points on the ground plane, in order around the area, that does not cross itself; it is always closed. The agent writes one with markup_create (kind: "region" and an outline); the outline is linear (drawn exactly as sent) unless type: "smooth" asks for a smooth curve through the points. A region stands extrudeHeight meters tall (1 to 100, 8 by default). Heights are ignored for what is inside: a region is an area of ground.

A region can list up to 32 members, other mark-ups it includes or excludes. Inside a region means inside its outline or any Include member, and not inside any Exclude member. A clearing in a forest is an Exclude member; a lake can be an Exclude member of the forest and of the village at once; a field joined to the forest is an Include member. A box counts its outline seen from above, a sphere the circle beneath it, and a member region its own outline only (its own members do not apply, so a list never loops). A region cannot list itself. When a member is deleted, the region skips it; undo brings it back as a member, and saving the scene drops it from the list. Duplicating a region together with its members gives a copy whose members are the copies; duplicating it alone gives a copy that shares its members.

markup_update with kind: "region" converts a box or a sphere to a region, as one undo step and one way: the title, description, status, tags and thread stay. A box with no outline keeps its outline seen from above and its height. With kind: "path" and points (and type) it converts a box or a sphere to a path the same way: a stand-in box for a road becomes the road and keeps its conversation.

Drawing a region yourself. Right-click the Mark-up tool in the Scene View's tool strip and choose Region. Drag around the area to paint a lasso: it closes where you release it, and its points are thinned to follow your stroke within 1 % of its size (between 0.5 and 5 m), so a 200 m lasso keeps one or a few dozen points, not hundreds. Or click the points one by one and press Enter, or click the first point again, to close the outline; Escape cancels. An outline that crosses itself is refused with a notice: draw it again without crossing. The new region is selected with its points showing (the spline tool); drag a point to move it and press Delete to remove the selected point, each one undo step. A region always stays closed: its spline's Closed setting cannot be unticked, and an outline read open from a scene file or written open through set_component is closed again, with a warning in the log.

The Region block of the inspector, in a region's Mark-up section above its thread, shows the outline's points, perimeter and Outline area (the area inside the outline before its members apply, in hectares from 10,000 m²), the height (dragging the slider previews the walls; releasing it is one undo step; the value beside it, in meters, takes a typed height from 1 to 100 m), Edit outline (selects the region and shows its points), and the Members list: every other mark-up whose outline overlaps the region, and every member, each with a checkbox (a member or not) and an Include | Exclude choice, the filled one the member's mode (Exclude in amber; neither filled for a mark-up that is not a member). Each change is one undo step. A member that was deleted reads "deleted (undo restores it)", one that no longer overlaps "outside the region". A box's Mark-up Volume section has Convert to region: the box becomes a region with the box's outline from above and its height, as one undo step.

What a region looks like. A region draws as a translucent wall standing on the ground along its outline, its height tall, with a lid that follows the ground at the wall's height, so on a slope it reads as a band of even height. The walls stand on the terrain, not on the houses and trees on it, and where the ground is under the sea they stand on the sea surface. The rim brightens along the wall's top edge. The title's pill stands over the point inside the region farthest from its outline, so it stays inside even a C-shaped region. While the outline crosses itself the walls stand and the lid is left out; with fewer than three points it draws as a line. Its row in the Mark-ups panel says so ("crosses itself", "outline needs three points"), and the inspector names the fix. The members shape what a region draws: an Exclude member cuts a hole through the lid, with walls standing around the hole, and an Include member extends the walls and the lid over it (a sphere's circle drawn as a 32-sided outline). Moving, deleting or restoring a member redraws the regions that list it. While the outline crosses itself the members are not applied.

Paths

A path is a way along the ground: a river, a road, the route to the guards' base. It is a spline of 2 to 256 points that is never closed, with no area: markup_contains refuses a path. The agent writes one with markup_create (kind: "path" and points, world [x, y, z] in meters in order along the way; type: "smooth" for a curve through them, linear by default). Each point is stood on the terrain under it, so the points Edit path offers are on the line you see; y counts only off the terrain. It draws as a glowing band with a line along its middle, lying on the terrain across its whole width, and the band keeps a few pixels of width at any distance (at least 1.5 m to either side, wider far from the camera); its title's pill stands halfway along it. The Mark-up section of the inspector shows its Path block: its points, its length along the ground, and Edit path, which selects it and shows its points in the spline tool to drag, add and delete. markup_update changes a path's title, status, notes, tags and color; it refuses points (move them with Edit path).

What you see in the Scene View

Each mark-up draws as a translucent volume in its status color with an outline. On the desktop editor the volume glows: a see-through body with a rim in its color, along a sphere's silhouette and along a box's edges. In the browser editor the volume is a flat translucent fill instead. Either way, the part behind terrain or other geometry draws dimmed, so a mark-up stays findable from the far side of a hill. A pill above the volume shows its title beside a dot in the status color.

  • Highlight: the selected mark-up draws a stronger fill (on the desktop, a stronger rim) and a doubled outline, and its pill a border in the accent color. The one under the pointer in the Mark-ups panel draws its outline in its full status color (on the desktop, with a denser body and a rim between the resting and the selected one), and its pill a lighter plate bordered in that color. Both pills draw over the others.
  • Inside a volume: with the camera inside a mark-up (for a region, inside its outline and below its top), it draws its outline only, and a volume's pill stays at the top of the view.
  • Updates: a mark-up the agent changed since you last looked has a red ring on the corner of its label, the Activity badge's color. Selecting it marks it seen.
  • Framing: double-click a row in the Mark-ups panel to frame the whole volume.
  • Labels: a title shows while the volume is at least about 1/100 of a radian across from the camera (a 20 m box until about 1.5 km away, a 2 m box until about 170 m), for at most the 64 nearest mark-ups. A pill whose point is out of view is held at the edge of the view, clear of the axis widget.
  • Many mark-ups: the 64 nearest the camera (and the selected ones) draw a body (the glow or the fill); the rest draw their outline only.
  • Hidden mark-ups draw nothing, and nothing draws in play mode. The Scene View's gizmo toggle hides them with the other gizmos; an agent hides only them with set_gizmos_visibility and markups: false.

Limits

  • A mark-up belongs to a scene. One inside a blueprint or a subscene is refused when the scene loads, with a message that says to move it into the scene that places the instance.
  • Mark-ups are edited outside play mode. In play mode the Mark-up tool places nothing and the Mark-up and Mark-up Volume inspector sections are read-only, with a notice to stop play first; hiding and showing mark-ups in the Mark-ups panel still works.
  • Duplicating a mark-up copies its shape, title and status; the copy starts its own, empty thread.
  • Hiding a mark-up is your view, not a scene edit: it does not dirty the scene and is not undone. The editor remembers hidden mark-ups and what you have seen, per project and per scene. A mark-up you hide before the scene is first saved is remembered for the next session once the scene has been saved.

The agent's methods

An agent reaches mark-ups through the editor's debug port: as MCP tools, or from a shell with node mcp/ge.mjs <method> --help for each method's parameters. A method that cannot act answers with an error that names the fix, and every method refuses in play mode.

Reading what changed

Every answer carries the scene's revision, a number that grows with every change to a mark-up. (A row's own revision is the mark-up's: the scene revision of its last change.) To see what the user did since your last look, pass the revision your previous answer returned as since with changedBy: "User". since is strict and is not a clock, so two changes in the same second are never lost. Every answer also carries an epoch; when it differs from the one your last answer carried, the scene was reopened or replaced, so list everything again instead of using since.

Acting as the user

Writes the port makes count as the agent's. A scripted demonstration that plays the user's part passes author: "User" to markup_create, markup_update or markup_comment.

A plan in five steps

1. The agent marks the plan: markup_create for each place, with a description and status Proposed, then a comment asking you to adjust it.
2. You drag and scale the shapes, comment, and set the ones that are right to Requested.
3. The agent calls markup_list with since and changedBy: "User", reads your changes with markup_get, and answers in the thread.
4. The agent sets a mark-up to InProgress, does the work with the usual scene tools, and sets it to Complete with a closing comment.
5. You review, and accept or set Revision with a comment. Either side can set Problem when a step is stuck.
