Spline Walls

Feature guide · level authoring / splines

A Spline Wall is a wall you draw. Place a spline, add the Spline Wall component to it, and a wall of the thickness and height you set stands along the spline as one continuous mesh. It has no kit pieces, so there are no joins and nothing stretches. A corner point on a spline of straight segments gives a sharp mitred corner. A curved spline gives a curved wall. One setting makes the corners round instead. The texture runs along the wall in metres, so a 10 m wall and a 100 m wall show stones of the same size.

Draw a wall

1. Create a spline and place its points. Set the spline's Type to Linear for straight walls with sharp corners, or keep a curved type for a curved wall.
2. Select the spline and add the Spline Wall component. A 0.6 m thick, 3 m tall gray wall appears along the spline and stands on the ground under it.
3. To close a courtyard, set the spline to Closed. The wall runs from the last point back to the first and meets itself with no seam.
4. Assign a Material to give the wall its stone. Set Thickness and Height to taste.

Nothing else to set

A plain wall needs no settings. The spline's width does not affect the wall; its thickness is the component's Thickness. Edits rebuild the wall a quarter of a second after you stop dragging.

Corners

A corner is an authored point on a Linear spline, or a point on a curved spline where the curve turns sharply, by more than 5 degrees (a Bezier point whose two handles are broken). Between corners a curved spline is always swept as the curve it is, whatever the Corner setting says.

Mitre

The two faces meet on one sharp vertical edge, and the top meets on the mitre line, the way a masonry corner does. This is what a wall on a Linear spline starts with.

Round

The outside of the corner sweeps an arc, and the inside meets at a point. Use it for earthworks, hedges and rounded bastions. This is what a wall on a curved spline starts with.

Adding the component picks the corner from the spline's type at that moment. Changing the spline's type afterwards leaves your choice alone, and resetting the component restores the values it had when you first selected it, not a corner picked again from the type.

Turns sharper than 120 degrees are always round

A mitred corner on a very sharp turn would reach out toward infinity, so a turn past 120 degrees is built round even with Corner set to Mitre, and the log says so, naming the point. Its inside meets where the two inside faces cross. On a leg too short to reach that point, the inside narrows toward the corner instead.

Slopes

The wall's base always stands in the ground: on a cross slope the base sinks so its downhill edge meets the ground, which leaves its uphill edge buried a little. Nothing floats.

Racked

The top runs parallel to the ground, as a hedge or a palisade does. This is the default.

Stepped

The top of each stretch between two points is level, at Height above that stretch's highest ground, and the wall steps up or down where two stretches meet, as coursed masonry does. No part of the wall is shorter than Height; it grows taller toward the downhill end of each stretch. Put a point wherever you want a step.

Materials

With no material assigned the wall is a flat gray, so it is visible on the first try. Two kinds of material give it stone.

A material that tiles in metres

Any material with a tiling texture works. The wall's texture coordinates are in metres, so the material's own tiling sets the stone size: a texture that covers 2 m of wall needs a tiling of 0.5. The coordinates are laid out so a coursed texture reads right:

  • Along each side face, the texture runs the face's own length. The outside of a corner or a bend is longer than the inside, and each face tiles at the same size, so stones at a corner match stones mid-wall.
  • Up the faces, the texture runs in world height above the wall's lowest point, so courses stay level on a slope, the top cuts across them, and they meet across every corner and every step.
  • On a closed wall each face closes on itself on a whole metre, so the texture has no seam where the loop meets.
  • The top runs along the wall and across its width. At a mitred corner the top's joints stay square to each leg and meet on the mitre line. On top of a round corner the pattern runs around the outer edge at its true size and gathers to a point at the inside corner, as coping stones do.

A world-space material

A material whose surface shader is the triplanar one projects its texture in world space and ignores the wall's texture coordinates. The same material then continues across the wall and across any kit pieces beside it. On a wall that runs diagonally to the world axes, a coursed texture shows its courses from two projections, so prefer the metre-tiling material for coursed stone and brick.

What the log tells you

Each problem is logged once, naming the wall and the point, with the fix. Rebuilding the same wall does not repeat it.

MessageWhat to do
A point turns past the 120-degree mitre limit, so the corner is built round. Widen the turn to keep a mitred corner.
A point is too close to the end of the wall for its corner. Lengthen the end leg or thin the wall.
Two points turn the same way too close together. Lengthen the leg between them or thin the wall.
The curve between two points bends tighter than half the wall's thickness, so the inside face folds there. Widen the curve or thin the wall.
Thickness or Height is below 0.05 m. Set at least 0.05 m; the wall is built at that size until you do.

Limits

  • The wall is built in the editor. It is saved with the scene as its settings, not as a mesh, and a game built from the scene does not generate it.
  • The wall has no collider yet: characters and physics objects pass through it.
  • Towers, gates and battlements are not part of the wall. Place kit pieces for them by hand.
  • A spline longer than 4096 m is followed less closely past that length, and the log says so.
